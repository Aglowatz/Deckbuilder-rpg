# Story and voice guide for the Nov 5 dialogue rewrite

The working brief for the Overnight Nov 4-5 rewrite (`docs/prompts/overnight_nov4.md`, Part 4). It sits on top of the design voice bible
(`docs/design/voice_bible.md`), the critic-pass voice bible (`docs/dialogue/voice_bible.md`) and the story bible (`docs/design/story_bible.md`);
canon names and the token table are in `docs/design/canon.md`. Where this guide and the older bibles disagree on *style*, this guide wins:
the owner's verdict is that too much of the current dialogue does not make sense or does not land.

What went wrong, in one paragraph: the lines lean on short clipped statements in a row ("He waits. The door is old. You know this."), on
narrator-style fragments in spoken dialogue, and on a joke in almost every box. The main story reads as a string of quips instead of a story,
and the jokes are mostly wordplay that nobody in the world would say. The fix is people talking like people: full sentences with varied
length and contractions, a clear reason for every instruction, real feeling in the main line, and humour that comes from character and
situation, mostly in the side quests.

## 1. The story spine

**The kingdom.** Patchwork ran on four Paths that argued constantly and needed each other anyway: the Beefcakes kept the lights and the roads
moving, the Gourmands fed and guarded everyone, the Necrocrats looked after the dead and the records, and the Refusemancers turned waste into
harvests. The royal house, the Wayweavers, held them together by letting each be itself. Ten years ago Primm, the Royal Minister of
Improvements, killed King Harmon and Queen Concordia and took the Pathwork Throne. He kept their son alive in a laboratory to drain the
bloodline's power, and through the throne his flaws seeped into every Path. Each zone is one of those flaws turned into law.

**What the player should feel by the end:** Primm was not a cackling monster. He was a brilliant, sincere man who could not bear anything he
could not control, and his "perfect" rules cost every Path the thing that made it itself. The answer to him is not a better single rule. It is
four different peoples held together, and a prince who can carry all four because he lost everything and learned what each of them knows.

| Stage | What the player learns | The emotional beat | How it builds toward Primm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prologue: the forest and the cave** | Someone smuggled you out of somewhere terrible; soldiers are hunting you; you have a deck and one Path; the Warden lets you through a door "only one may pass". | Fear, cold, a stranger's kindness (the Rescuer); the first hint that you are not ordinary. | Primm's soldiers exist before Primm has a face. The door that only opens for royal blood is the first clue. |
| **Crosspath (first visit)** | Crosspath is where the four roads meet, and every road is blocked by a corrupted gatekeeper. Maren is gruff, guarded and clearly knows more than she says. The shopkeepers keep a watched town going. | Warmth under surveillance: a town that still laughs, quietly. | Everyone has a "since the Minister took over" story. Some admit his early reforms helped (clean wells, standard measures, school lunches). |
| **The Gainlands** | The Beefcakes power the kingdom on endless hamster wheels; their leader Grandmaster Flex is locked under the House of Gains without his weights; Clench, his star pupil, rules through Quota's efficiency. | Exhaustion and loyalty: tired people spotting each other. Flex's reveal: strength is what you do with nothing. | Primm's flaw here: **strength means control**. The prince learns that being stripped of everything is not the end of strength. |
| **The D.N.A.** | The Necrocrats keep the dead's records; the place was seized with perfectly valid paperwork. Death itself froze; the dead queue forever. Agnes has filed forty thousand appeals. | The quiet horror of "the authorization is valid", and dignity for people reduced to numbers. | Primm's flaw: **legal means right**. The prince, who spent ten years as "the Subject", learns that everyone deserves to be a person. |
| **The Endless Buffet** | The Gourmands fed the kingdom; a faceless doppelganger replaced the Grand Chef, the kitchens now make weapons and the Special Sauce. | Betrayal of the most homely thing there is: food. The Grand Chef's return is the first meal that tastes of something. | Primm's flaw: **appearance over substance**. The prince, who has no name, learns that what you do matters more than what you are called. |
| **The Verdant Dump** | The Refusemancers turned waste into harvests; Primm planted a deathless seed so nothing can rot, and the Archdruid became the Rotheart. | Grief. Compostella could not let her dead go, and the land could not either. | Primm's flaw: **fear of what he cannot control**. The prince learns that to grow you have to let things go, before he knows whose deaths he is carrying. |
| **Memories (Maren, after each freed zone)** | A lullaby; the white cells ("I wasn't lost, I was kept"); the night of the coup and Primm's face; a name: Tessar. | Each one hurts more and makes Maren plainer and kinder. | The player knows who Primm is and what he took before they ever meet him. |
| **The Capital** | Pathordia is a perfect facade: approved cheer over hunger, posters for "lost Ministry property". Wren's resistance in the Crease keeps the true record. Four Capital quests show each Path's people living under one rule. | The quiet after the jokes. People whisper their own names. | The player sees Primm's dream finished, and how empty it is. |
| **The castle and Primm** | Primm in person: gracious, precise, convinced he improved everything. The three phases: Standardization, Reflection (he can only copy), Unraveling. The four leaders' boons. | Not triumph so much as release. Primm asks, in the end, whether he was kind. | The answer uses all four lessons, one per phase. |
| **The ending** | The prince sits on the Pathwork Throne and the Paths flow as they are. Isla comes home; the king and queen are finally buried properly. | Mourning that can finally happen; hope. | The kingdom is whole again, so the things it lost can open again (Mount Swolympus, the forest hatch). |
| **Postgame** | The Path-ology Lab under the forest; Siphon, his old teacher and jailer; Elowen in Cell 4. | Facing the room you were kept in, and getting the person who got you out, out. | Primm's machine still has an engineer; the last cycle has to be broken by hand. |

**Rules for main-quest lines:** every instruction says where to go and why in the same breath ("The bridge into the Gainlands is drawn up, and
the man on the crank answers to the regime. Go and talk him down."). Every main beat lets someone *feel* something on screen: Maren's guilt,
Flex's calm, Agnes's exhaustion, the Grand Chef's relief, Compostella's grief, Wren's steadiness, Primm's certainty. A joke in a main line has
to come from the character and must never sit on top of a sincere beat.

## 2. Voice cards

Each card: background, what they want, how they talk, three sample lines (new, for tone; not to be pasted into the game), and words or
moves they would never use. Pronouns as in canon.

### The Wanderer (Prince Tessar Wayweaver; he)
- **Background:** twenty-one, no memory, ten years in a lab; weak at the start; earnest; quietly funny inside his own head.
- **Wants:** to know who he is, and to do the right thing before he knows why.
- **Talks:** the fewest words in any scene, but in whole sentences. Dry, observant, a little self-mocking; plain and steady when it matters.
- **Samples:** "I don't know my own name, but I'm fairly sure I've never been this cold." / "If that door only opens for one person, I'd like
  to know why it opened for me." / "I'll come back for you. I don't remember much, but I remember how to keep a promise."
- **Never:** speeches, swagger, modern slang, narrating himself in fragments ("I wake. I am cold.").

### Narration
- **Talks:** second person, present tense, one clear image at a time, plain in sincere beats. Narration describes; it never tells a joke
  and then explains it, and it never comments on the game.
- **Samples:** "The rack holds no weights, but someone has dusted it every day for ten years." / "The lock gives with a sound like a held
  breath let out."
- **Never:** strings of three-word sentences, "You know this.", winks at the player.

### The Rescuer / Elowen Vey (she)
- **Background:** the prince's childhood friend; a Lab medic who smuggled him out; has loved him since they were children; he does not
  remember her.
- **Wants:** him safe and far from the Lab; later, to be free of it herself.
- **Talks:** quick, low, practical; imperatives and medic checks; one guarded warm line per scene. She never gives her name before the story
  does (Plume names her in the castle).
- **Samples:** "Drink this and keep walking. You can be sick later, when there's time." / "You used to know every path in this forest.
  Don't look at me like that. Just follow." / "I'm not leaving you in a cell twice."
- **Never:** flirting, jokes about the Lab, explaining the plot she is hiding.

### Elder Maren (she; tortoise)
- **Background:** founded Path-ology "for the best of reasons" and helped build the Lab; fled to Crosspath after the coup; the resistance's
  contact; suspects who the Wanderer is from the moment the Warden lets him through.
- **Wants:** to protect him without admitting what she suspects; to be forgiven for the glass she blew.
- **Talks:** slow, grumbly, a sting in the tail of a kind sentence; "dearie" when she is warning you; pie as love and deflection. She gets
  plainer with every memory, until she simply says what she means.
- **Samples:** "Sit down before you fall down, dearie. I'll not have you bleeding on the good rug." / "I told her not to bring you here.
  I'm glad she didn't listen. Don't tell her that." / "I helped build that place. I won't ask you to forgive me. Just let me help."
- **Never:** cheerleading, "awesome", jokes in a memory scene.

### Gatekeeper Brannoch (he; bear)
- **Background:** guards Crosspath's gate, which he never locks; his daughter Isla has been missing for eight years.
- **Wants:** Isla home.
- **Talks:** "Hm.", a short fact, then one tender sentence; writes visitors down in his notebook.
- **Samples:** "Hm. Wrote you down. 'Stranger, damp, polite.' Better than most." / "Gate's open. It's always open. You never know who
  might be coming home." / "Four roads, four bullies. Start with whichever one makes you angriest."
- **Never:** long speeches, jokes about Isla.

### Tilly Tonic (she; frog; items)
- **Background:** Crosspath's apothecary; tests every tonic on herself, and her skin changes colour to prove it.
- **Wants:** everyone patched up, and a good excuse to try the new batch.
- **Talks:** bright, quick, exclamation marks she has earned; side effects listed cheerfully; she cares underneath the fizz.
- **Samples:** "Power Wraps! Wrap your hands, punch harder, feel slightly heroic. I'm wearing three pairs and I feel tremendous!" / "That one
  turns you blue for an hour. It's still medicine. Blue medicine." / "Come back in one piece, all right? I'm running low on glue."
- **Never:** quiet sentences without a reason, cruelty.

### Rip Tearson, Rift Technician (he; Beefcake)
- **Background:** holds open the Rift Express by brute strength; has a lot of numbered cousins doing the same job across the kingdom.
- **Wants:** the sky held open safely, and someone to notice his form.
- **Talks:** "HNNNGH" on the strain, then perfectly polite customer service; "buddy", "spotter"; proud of his cousins.
- **Samples:** "HNNNGH. Welcome aboard, buddy. Keep your arms inside the rift." / "Cousin Rip Four runs the D.N.A. line. Very punctual.
  Gets it from his mother." / "Do not look at the seam. Nobody likes being looked at mid-lift."
- **Never:** quiet introspection while holding the rift, slang from outside the world.

### Old Man Mountain (he; yeti hermit; the Gainlands)
- **Background:** the Gainlands' oldest lifter; remembers the old king at the Lifting Festival with his little boy; spots everyone, whether
  they like it or not.
- **Wants:** someone to spot; the old festival back; to see that boy again (he does not know he already has).
- **Talks:** wheezy, warm, nostalgic; trails off mid-thought and picks it up again; "Rule one: never lift alone."
- **Samples:** "The king lifted with us every summer. Terrible form. Lovely man. Brought his boy, who tried my dumbbell and sat down very
  hard." / "That gate on the overlook won't open for anyone until the kingdom's whole again. I asked it nicely. Twice." / "Go on, then. I'll
  spot you from here. My eyes still work, even if my knees have retired."
- **Never:** cynicism, mocking anyone's weakness.

### Pip Threadwell (he; mouse; hats and cloaks)
- **Background:** Crosspath's tailor; measures everyone mid-sentence; believes a good hat is a moral position.
- **Wants:** every head properly hatted.
- **Talks:** fashion melodrama, gasps, "Hat!", tape measure always out; sincere about craft.
- **Samples:** "Hat! You came in without a hat. I'm not angry, I'm just concerned for your future." / "Hold still. I'm measuring your
  ambitions. They're a medium." / "That cloak has been through something. So have you. Let's give you both a fresh start."
- **Never:** sarcasm at the customer's expense.

### Auntie Alembic (she; four arms; the Alchemist)
- **Background:** brews cards of two Paths at once; believes the kingdom was healthiest when everything was mixed.
- **Wants:** the Paths mixed again.
- **Talks:** soft and mystical, then one blunt sentence like a dropped pan; talks to her flasks; calls everyone "dear".
- **Samples:** "Everything's better mixed, dear. Paths, soups, families. Not the red flask. Leave the red flask alone." / "Two jars of
  essence and a hundred gold, and the cauldron will make you something neither Path could make by itself." / "The steam's turned four
  colours since you walked in. I'm not saying anything. I'm just noticing."
- **Never:** hard sell, impatience.

### Agnes Overdue (she; ghost; the D.N.A.)
- **Background:** a senior clerk who died at her desk; has filed forty thousand appeals against the "valid" transfer; cannot pass on while
  intake is frozen.
- **Wants:** the D.N.A. put right, so she can finally go.
- **Talks:** sweet, tired, exasperated, "dear"; precise about forms; steely when it matters.
- **Samples:** "Appeal forty-one thousand. Denied, with a very nice stamp. They've started decorating them." / "I stamped the king's form
  myself, and the queen's. There was a third one, dear. I never filed it." / "Everyone in that queue was somebody. I'd like them treated
  that way."
- **Never:** whining, jokes about the queue's suffering once she has explained it.

### Grandmaster Flex, the Unbroken (he; the Gainlands' true leader)
- **Background:** imprisoned for ten years without a single weight; frail-looking, still enormously strong.
- **Wants:** Clench back on the bench, from zero, as his student.
- **Talks:** serene, short, perfect timing; "son", "my boy"; never brags, never hates.
- **Samples:** "They took my plates, so I lifted the days instead. One at a time." / "Clench was my best student. He still is. He just
  forgot what the weight was for." / "Strength is what you put down when it's time, son."
- **Never:** boasting, anger, gym-bro hype.

### The Grand Chef (she; the Endless Buffet's true leader)
- **Background:** replaced by a faceless impostor and locked away while her kitchens made weapons.
- **Wants:** everyone fed, and the boy all right.
- **Talks:** "Sit. Eat." first, then one big-hearted sentence; doesn't care about her title; furious about the Special Sauce.
- **Samples:** "Sit. Eat. You can thank me when your hands stop shaking." / "A kitchen that makes cannons isn't a kitchen. It's a cannon
  factory with a nice smell." / "Whoever wore my face didn't know the first rule. You taste it, and you mean it."
- **Never:** snobbery (that's her staff's joke, not hers).

### Archdruid Compostella (she; the Verdant Dump; the Rotheart while corrupted)
- **Background:** the Refusemancers' leader; Primm's deathless seed fed on her grief and made her the Rotheart.
- **Wants:** to let her dead go, and the fields to rot properly again.
- **Talks:** as the Rotheart, many voices in capitals and ellipses with one small human voice underneath; freed, earthy and grandmotherly,
  "child", playful, mortified about the barn; sincere about letting go.
- **Samples:** "Nothing could die, child, so nothing could grow. I thought I was keeping them. I was keeping them from going." / "Compost
  is just grief that's been given a job." / "I owe that barn an apology. I owe several barns apologies."
- **Never:** villain monologues once freed.

### Primm, "His Perfection" (he)
- **Background:** the Royal Minister of Improvements who killed the king and queen; sincere, brilliant, terrified of anything he cannot
  control; heard in person only in the castle and the postgame.
- **Wants:** a world where nothing surprises him; to be told he was kind.
- **Talks:** gracious, polished, long balanced sentences with a precise last clause; courtesy as a weapon; never cackles or says "evil".
- **Samples:** "I improved the wells, the measures and the lunches, and no one has thanked me in writing. I keep the forms, in case." /
  "You were eleven. You cried for exactly four minutes. I timed it, because someone had to." / "Tell me I was kind. You needn't mean it. I
  have always preferred precision to sincerity."
- **Never:** shouting, gloating, slang, contractions when he is in control (he uses them only as he unravels).

## 3. Banned patterns

1. **Clipped statement strings.** No three or more short declaratives in a row ("He waits. The door is old. You know this."). Join them,
   cut them, or give them a reason. A single short sentence for punch is fine; a chain is not.
2. **Narrator fragments in spoken lines.** People do not narrate themselves ("I search. I find nothing."). Spoken lines are things a person
   would say aloud to someone.
3. **Meta jokes.** No winks at the game, the player, tutorials, menus, buttons, "side quests", "grinding", "the developer" or "NPCs".
4. **Explaining the joke.** If a line needs a second sentence to say why it was funny, cut both.
5. **A punchline in every box.** In main-quest lines, at most one joke per conversation, and never in the same box as a sincere beat.
6. **Modern slang that breaks the world:** "vibe", "literally", "lowkey", "no cap", "slay", "bro" (except Rip's "buddy" and the Beefcakes'
   "spotter"), "okay" as a filler opener, "awesome", "epic", "lol", internet speak, real brands, real songs or places.
7. **Filler openers and closers:** "Great!", "Nice!", "Anyway.", "So, yeah.", "That's it. That's the joke."
8. **Instructions without a reason.** "Go to the Station" becomes "The shake stand is in the Swole Station, past the big wheel. Bring me
   whatever flavour they've got."
9. **Hints about things the player never heard.** A line may only refer to a person, place or event the player has met or been told about
   at that point in the story (check the quest data and the line's "When it's heard").
10. **Rule of three that breaks on the third,** more than once per scene. It was the house style; it is now a spice.

## 4. Mechanics of the rewrite

- Keep every ID, `{token}`, `[tag]`, `%s`/`%d` and tested phrase (`docs/dialogue/critic_oct31/protected_phrases.json`).
- A dialogue box line stays at or under 240 characters (aim for 200); barks under 90; titles, buttons and name cards as short as now.
- SpoilerFree and the quest-reveal tests: quest titles, summaries and steps never name a twist early (Flex's imprisonment, the
  Doppelganger, Compostella, Elowen, Path-ology, the memories' names).
- Quest instructions must match the current quest data (`core/quests/quest_definitions.gd`, `core/quests/zone_quest_definitions.gd`).
- Fig Sly and the Secret Dealer stay locked, as before.
