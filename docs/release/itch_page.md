## Short pitch (the one line under the title, about 120 characters)

A cozy deckbuilding RPG: wake with no name, walk four strange lands, and win back a kingdom one card duel at a time.

Two alternatives:
- Build a deck, walk four absurd kingdoms and out-argue their corrupted rulers at cards. A free story RPG of 12 to 20 hours.
- A hand-painted card-battle RPG about a kingdom that only works when its four quarrelling halves stop matching.

## Longer description

**You wake in a forest with no memory, a deck of cards, and soldiers looking for you.**

The kingdom of Pathwork once ran on four Paths that argued constantly and needed each other anyway. The Beefcakes kept the lights on with giant
hamster wheels. The Gourmands fed everyone. The Necrocrats kept the records of the dead. The Refusemancers turned rubbish into
harvests. Then Primm, the Royal Minister of Improvements, took the throne and "perfected" them all, and each land became its own flaw
turned into law.

Walk the Gainlands, the Department of Necrotic Affairs, the Endless Buffet and the Verdant Dump. Out-muscle, out-file, out-cook and
out-compost their corrupted leaders in tactical card duels. Build your deck from a few hundred cards, free the four Paths, take the
road to the Capital, and find out who you were before the forest.

The Wayward Path is a single-player story game of about 12 to 20 hours, with a postgame after the ending. It is **free; donations are
welcome and never needed.**

## Features

- **The four Paths.** Beefcake hits hard and fast and burns Iron for bursts of strength. Gourmand cooks Ingredients into golems and
  second helpings. Necrocrat ties the enemy up in Red Tape and brings its own units back from the Refuse Pile. Refusemancer turns
  Garbage into growth. You start on one Path and earn the right to mix them as the story goes: a second Path once you free your first
  zone, all four after the ending.
- **Card duels on a real board.** Units, spells, traps, tools and wonders; a blocker you choose; keywords like Hustle, Bulldoze and
  Sucker Punch explained on hover and in a glossary. Bosses have scripted events that are announced a turn ahead, never sprung on you.
- **Four zones and a capital to walk.** Each has its own people, music, shop, side quests, a minigame (a classroom quiz, a gym rep
  counter, a kitchen rush, a sorting belt), a side dungeon and a main dungeon with a boss.
- **Dungeons with choices.** Node maps with fights, treasure, challenges and choice rooms: your HP carries from fight to fight, and a
  resting place heals a little for a price.
- **Crafting and collecting.** About 330 cards. Open packs, buy singles, and take spare copies to Auntie Alembic, who brews two-Path
  cards from their essence. A Collection Book tracks what you have found; a bestiary tracks who you have met.
- **A Deck Station** with saved deck slots, filters by Path, a search box and hints about what your deck is missing.
- **The Arena.** Four tiers of the Grand Clashatorium under the town: set-piece battles with special rules and "win this turn"
  puzzles, each with one proven solution.
- **A postgame.** After the ending: the Path-ology Lab under the forest, a rescue, the Shattered Gallery, Mount Swolympus, and three-
  and four-Path cards for your decks.
- **Gear, items and hats.** Five equipment slots, single-use items, and a Wardrobe of hats, cloaks and dyes that change nothing but
  how you look.
- **A painted look and an orchestral score,** with a theme for every place (see "About the art, music and writing").
- **Comfort options.** Full controller support, every key rebindable, a Text size switch (Normal, Large, Larger) for dialogue, cards,
  tooltips and menus, Path symbols for colour-blind players (on by default), Reduce motion, Reduce screen shake, Reduce flashing,
  five camera presets, autosave with a visible mark, three campaigns with five save slots each, and a Low-end preset that weak PCs
  choose by themselves.
- **No microtransactions, no online account, nothing to unlock with money.** Family friendly.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Move (sprint) | WASD or arrow keys (hold Shift) | left stick (click it) |
| Talk / use / enter | E or Space, or click | A |
| Map | M | View / Back |
| Quest log, tracked quest previous / next | J, [ and ] | D-pad left, LB / RB |
| Deck Station / Character / Packs / Wardrobe | B / C / P / T | X / Y / D-pad right / D-pad down |
| Camera: next preset, zoom, turn | V, F, hold Z or X | click the right stick, -, triggers |
| Battle: play a card | drag it onto the board, or click it and then its target | choose it and press A |
| Battle: look at a card | right-click or hover | Y |
| Battle: next phase / end turn | Space | X |
| Dungeon rules | R | Y |
| Pause menu, settings, glossary | Escape | Start |
| Report a problem | F8 | pause menu |

Every key can be changed in Settings > Controls.

## System requirements

Measured Nov 21 on the development PC (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 5 4500U with its integrated Radeon graphics, 7.4 GB RAM, 1600 x 900):
60 frames a second or more everywhere on the Medium preset (63 to 83 in the zones and the Capital, 74 in the town, about 350 in
a battle); the Low-end preset, which a weak PC picks by itself, is capped at 30 and has room to spare. The game uses about 0.8 GB of
RAM in menus and battles and 1.3 to 1.7 GB in the open world, plus 0.6 to 0.9 GB of video memory (shared with RAM on integrated
graphics). A zone loads in 4 to 6 seconds behind a loading screen. No leak over eight zone round trips.

- **OS:** Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
- **Minimum:** integrated graphics with DirectX 12 or Vulkan (Intel UHD 620 class or newer), **4 GB RAM**, a dual-core CPU from 2015
  or later. The game picks its Low-end preset by itself on a weak PC (30 frames a second). For very old graphics chips there is
  "Start (Compatibility).bat" (OpenGL).
- **Recommended:** **8 GB RAM** and any dedicated graphics card from the last eight years (an estimate: only the integrated-graphics
  PC was measured).
- **Disk:** about 950 MB unpacked.
- **Download:** about 770 MB zipped (last build: 768 MB; fill in the exact size after the final export)
