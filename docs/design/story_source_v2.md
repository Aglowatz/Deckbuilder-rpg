Canonical names, roles and node layouts also live in `data/source/npc_list.csv` and `data/source/dungeon_list.csv` (exported from the designer's Google Sheet).

## Overview

A stolen prince with no memory reclaims a kingdom whose strength was always its differences. Ten years ago Primm, the Royal Minister of Improvements, killed the king and queen and seized the Pathwork Throne. He kept their son alive to drain the bloodline's power. Now the prince has escaped. Every zone he frees loosens Primm's grip, returns a piece of his memory, and teaches him something he will need to rule.

**Theme:** one way for everyone breaks a kingdom; many ways, held together, make it whole. Primm demands sameness. The royal bloodline's gift is wielding all four Paths at once, as they are.

**Tone:** comedic on the surface, serious underneath. Every joke has a cost the player can see, and the jokes get quieter the closer the player gets to Primm.

## The Old Kingdom and the royal bloodline

The kingdom of **Pathavia** ran on four Paths, each providing a vital service. They argued constantly and needed each other anyway.

| Path | Service | Zone | Rival |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beefcakes | Energy and transport | The Gainlands | Necrocrats (chaos vs. by-the-book) |
| Gourmands | Food and protection | The Endless Buffet | Refusemancers (snooty vs. "garbage eaters") |
| Necrocrats | Death administration | The Department of Necrotic Affairs (D.N.A.) | Beefcakes |
| Refusemancers | Waste removal and agriculture | The Verdant Dump | Gourmands |

**The Pathwork Throne** sits in the Capital, where the four Paths meet. Whoever sits on it is connected to all four, and the ruler's character flows out through them into the land.

**The royal bloodline** can wield all four Paths at once. That is why the throne worked under them: they carried the Paths as they were, without forcing them into one shape.

**House Wayweaver** is the royal family: they weave the four Paths together without forcing them into one. **King Harmon Wayweaver** was a gentle, patient ruler who held the Paths together by letting each be itself. **Queen Concordia Wayweaver** was clever, musical, and fierce; hers is the lullaby the prince remembers first, and she died shielding him in the coup. Their son is **Prince Tessar Wayweaver**, named for the tesserae of a mosaic: many different pieces making one picture.

**The royal crest** is the four Path symbols in a ring. It appears in the world without explanation: Elder Maren's walking stick, the Forgotten Vault's Old Kingdom banner, and the Vault Guardian's four-colored core. Each is a quiet clue to who the Wanderer is.

## Primm, "His Perfection"

Primm was the Royal Minister of Improvements: arrogant, selfish, extremely bright, and sincere. He believed the kingdom would be better under his rule, and he saw it as his official duty to improve it by any means. That included removing the royal family. Greed and ambition blinded him to the difference between improving the kingdom and owning it.

**The coup.** Primm killed King Harmon and Queen Concordia and took the Pathwork Throne. He spared their son, because he wanted the power in the royal bloodline for himself.

**How the kingdom was corrupted.** Sitting on the Pathwork Throne connected Primm to all four Paths, and his darker traits poured through them. Each faction's corruption is one of his flaws turned into policy:

| Path | What happened | Primm's flaw behind it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beefcakes | A coup; the leader imprisoned; a regime under Chancellor Clench | Strength means control |
| Gourmands | A doppelganger replaced the leader; corrupted food; kitchens turned into a weapons factory | Appearance over substance |
| Necrocrats | Authority transferred by perfectly valid paperwork; enforced by the CE-No | Legal means right |
| Refusemancers | The leader corrupted through nature itself; growth that will not stop | Fear of anything he cannot control |

**The Path-ologists** are the kingdom's scientists of the Paths. Elder Maren founded the field and led it before the coup. Under Primm, and under a new Chief Path-ologist (Dr. Ambrose Siphon), they spent ten years in a hidden lab in the forest (the Path-ology Lab), running the prince through grueling tests and trials to extract his innate power.

**The stolen power.** Primm has no royal blood, so his hold on the throne depends on the power drained from the prince. When the prince escaped, that hold started to slip. That's why the zones can be freed now, after ten years, and why rifts are tearing open in the Capital. It's also why his "Reflection" phase can only copy: his power was never his.

**Why he is tragic.** He was always sincere and truly brilliant. His early reforms really helped (clean wells, standard measures, school lunches), and some of his complaints about the old kingdom were valid. He cannot see that every improvement made the kingdom more like him, and that is what makes him impossible to trust.

## The Wanderer: the lost prince

The Wanderer is Prince Tessar Wayweaver, son of the murdered king and queen, presumed dead. He was eleven at the coup and is twenty-one now. Ten years in the Path-ology Lab plus the corruption flowing from the Pathwork Throne left him weak and amnesiac. A Path-ologist who defected smuggled him out, and he remembers none of it. In-game he is "The Wanderer" until his identity is revealed.

### Power returns as the land heals

| Point in the story | Paths he can wield | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start of game | One (the starting deck he chooses) | Weakened, and the corruption smothers his gift |
| After his first freed zone | Two at once | The corruption over the land wanes |
| After defeating Primm and taking the throne | All of them (3+ Path decks) | The throne answers to the true bloodline; his memory and power are fully restored |

### Memory returns zone by zone

Each time a zone is freed, the Wanderer gets a fragment back, revealed in a talk with Elder Maren. Fragments follow the NUMBER of zones freed, not which zone, so any order works.

1. **First zone: warmth.** Four-colored light, a woman humming, a hand on his head. Maren goes quiet: it's a lullaby from the old days. (Second Path unlocks here.)
2. **Second zone: the cells.** White rooms, glass, people in coats who call him "the Subject," the word "again." He realizes he was a prisoner. Maren starts trusting him and admits she works with the resistance.
3. **Third zone: the coup.** A throne room at night. A man in white and gold kneeling to the king, then standing. Two people pushing a boy behind them. He knows the man's face: Primm. Maren, shaken by the lab he describes, admits her own secret: she founded the study of the Paths and helped build that lab, then fled when Primm took over.
4. **Fourth zone: his name.** He is the royal prince, presumed dead. Maren finally recognizes the boy she once met at court, and understands what her lab was used for. If he fights Primm before this, Primm tells him instead.
5. **On the Pathwork Throne: everything.** Full memory and full power.

### What each zone teaches him

Each zone's story mirrors part of his own wound, so the four lessons are what he brings to the throne. Order doesn't matter.

| Zone | Its lesson | Why it hits him |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gainlands | Strength comes from the heart and mind | Flex was imprisoned and stripped too, and came out stronger |
| Endless Buffet | What you're called doesn't matter; what you do does | He doesn't know his name or who he is, only that what he's doing is right |
| D.N.A. | Everyone deserves humanity, even the dead; orders don't outrank respect | For ten years he was treated as a subject, not a person |
| Verdant Dump | Death isn't the end; to grow, you have to let things go | He has a grief he can't name yet: his parents |

## Prologue: the forest and the Forgotten Cave

1. **Waking.** The Wanderer wakes in a forest outside Crosspath with no idea who he is. A hooded stranger kneels beside him: his rescuer, a defected Path-ologist, though he doesn't know it. This is NOT Elder Maren.
2. **"Can you still fight?"** The rescuer asks if he remembers how to fight and pull energy from the Paths. He does, but barely. The rescuer notes how weak he is and asks which Path he walked the most. **The player chooses a starting deck here.**
3. **Sent on.** The rescuer points him to the Forgotten Cave ("the town is on the other side") and slips back into the forest. The player can't explore: if they wander off, the Wanderer talks himself back (he has to go through the cave to reach town).
4. **The Forgotten Cave** (renamed from Trial of the Hollow; it is a passage, not a trial). The tutorial dungeon, ending with the Hollow Warden. No Beefcake or Necrocrat infrastructure or resources appear in it.
5. **Elder Maren.** She is waiting at the cave mouth. Her contact inside the lab sent word that someone would be smuggled out through the old tunnel, but not who. She takes him in and shelters him, and she is slow to trust: he could be a spy. Kind, cryptic, full of pie, she gives him enough to start (the four Paths, the corrupted envoys, the gates) and keeps the rest.

**The cave's secret.** The Forgotten Cave is an old royal escape tunnel, and the Hollow Warden is an Old Kingdom guardian that only lets the royal bloodline pass. Maren told the rescuer about it, which is why the rescuer sent him through alone. At the start the Warden just grumbles that he "smells familiar"; after the fourth memory, the line lands. The Path-ology Lab's hidden entrance is in this same forest (inaccessible until the postgame), which is why he woke up here.

## The Gainlands: strength from the heart and mind

**Core lesson:** they took everything they thought made him strong, and missed the part that mattered.

**The situation.** Chancellor Clench, Iron Regent, runs the Gainlands for Primm like a bureaucracy of muscle. Beefcakes are worked on endless wheels to power the kingdom, and transport only runs where the regime approves. The rightful leader, Grandmaster Flex, rots in the Iron-less Prison beneath the House of Gains.

### Story beats

1. **Brick Bronson (town gate).** A courier barking "only iron matters." Freed, he's sheepish and has quietly taken up stretching, because of rumors about the old man in the prison who never broke.
2. **The zone.** Regime posters, wheels that never stop, Old Man Mountain wheezing about how it used to be. The comedy is gym-bro; underneath, people are exhausted.
3. **The House of Gains**, the regime's stronghold. The node where the leader is held is called **The Iron-less Prison** (an optional branch deep below).
4. **The Iron-less Prison.** Flex looks frail and ancient, sitting in meditation. He has spent ten years training with nothing: calisthenics, yoga, breath, discipline. The Wanderer is shaken: ten years, a cell, people trying to break him. He doesn't know why it feels familiar.
5. **Throne of Gains.** Clench sneers at the old man. Flex throws off his robes and reveals he is still enormously muscular. "They took my weights. They forgot to take me."
6. **After.** Flex to the Wanderer: "Whatever they took from you, son, it wasn't the part that matters."

**Clench's story.** Clench was Flex's star pupil, the strongest lifter he ever trained, who never believed he was strong without the iron. Primm offered him what he lacked: control. Flex beats him by refusing to hate him. After the fight Flex offers to train him again, from zero. Clench, who has lost his title, his medals, and his belief that strength is power, has to decide if he can start over with nothing.

**If Flex isn't rescued:** the boss is still beatable without his token. Afterward, Flex walks out of the prison on his own: "I was going to leave eventually. Lovely day for it."

**Freed state:** the wheels turn at a healthy pace, transport runs again, and Flex opens the gym to everyone.

## The Endless Buffet: what you do, not what you're called

**Core lesson:** what you're called doesn't matter. What you do is what matters.

**The situation.** The Endless Buffet looks like a thriving center of culinary invention. It is really a military-industrial complex. A doppelganger wearing Grand Chef Escoffina's face uses enchanted, corrupted food to bend the chefs, and the food golems that once protected the kingdom are being rebuilt as war machines for Primm.

### Story beats

1. **Chef Fennel Gravois (town gate).** Force-feeding passersby "for the war effort." Freed, he weeps about the old days, when Chef tasted every sauce herself.
2. **The zone.** Cheerful "innovation" signage, prototype tastings, golem guards. Chefs who flinch at the word "weapon."
3. **Basil's suspicion.** The nervous line cook has noticed: "Chef ALWAYS tastes her sauces. This one never does." His hook leads into the Test Kitchen.
4. **The Test Kitchen.** Prototypes, corrupted ingredients, unfinished war machines. In the R&D Archives the player finds proof of the swap.
5. **The Grand Kitchen.** The Doppelganger fights in Escoffina's face, then the disguise peels away into a faceless, shifting shape in a chef's coat. It has worn her face so long that it has forgotten its own.
6. **The real Grand Chef.** Escoffina is found in the deep freezer, where she's been kept "on ice" for ten years. With the impostor beaten, she is allowed back into her kitchen.
7. **The proof.** She bakes a wonder-filled dish that breaks the enchantment on the corrupted food. No speech or document could prove who she is; the dish does.
8. **The name.** The Doppelganger is lost and confused, with no face and no name of its own. Escoffina willingly gives it hers: "What you're called doesn't matter. It's what you do that matters." More than any title, she is proud of the work she has done and stands by it.

**Why it hits the Wanderer.** He doesn't know his name or who he is, only that what he's doing is right. Watching someone hand away her name and lose nothing that matters tells him that's enough to go on.

**Afterward.** The new Escoffina (the former Doppelganger) stays on in the kitchen, learning to cook honestly (a running postgame gag with real heart). Everyone else simply calls the true chef "Chef" ("The Grand Chef" in UI), which is all Basil ever called her anyway.

**Freed state:** the war machines are melted down into ovens, the golems go back to guarding pantries, and the Buffet feeds the kingdom again.

## The D.N.A.: even the dead deserve humanity

**Core lesson:** our job is to help the lost souls. Following orders be damned; we treat everyone with respect.

**The situation.** Authority over the Necrocrats was transferred to Primm through a process that was submitted, reviewed, approved, notarized, and filed. It is valid, so the Necrocrats obey. Mortimer Grimsby, CE-No, runs the Department for Primm and enforces every rule to the letter, no matter who it crushes. "Yes, but the authorization is valid."

### Story beats

1. **Prudence Pallor (town gate).** Stamping DENIED on everything in a monotone. Freed, she's tired, wry, and sorry.
2. **The zone.** Permits to stand, forms for forms, queue numbers for the queue. Gerald, Number 4,000,212, still waiting.
3. **Agnes Overdue.** A sweet, exasperated ghost who has waited so long to pass on that all she wants now is to let go. Every appeal she files is denied. She becomes the heart of this zone: she guides the player into the Hall with her enormous stack of appeals and turns up at its nodes, still filling out forms.
4. **The Hall of Final Approvals.** Departments, waiting rooms, Form 27-B. In the Records Labyrinth the player finds the original transfer document. It is flawless. At the Appeals Court, Undersecretary Vellum, Mortimer's deputy, defends it as an elite fight.
5. **Office of Final Approval.** Mortimer Grimsby, CE-No, is the boss. To the end he insists the authorization is valid and the procedure is correct. He has to go.
6. **The vacancy.** With the CE-No removed, Necrocrat policy says the office passes to the senior-most applicant on file. That's Agnes, who has filed more appeals than anyone alive or dead. All she wanted was to leave. She stays to fix the system first, so no one else suffers the way she did: "Even the dead deserve humanity. Our job is to help the lost souls. We have an obligation to help. Following orders be damned. We treat everyone with respect."

**Why it hits the Wanderer.** For ten years he was a subject in a file, not a person. Agnes is the first leader who says out loud that nobody should be treated that way.

**The loose thread.** The transfer document lists the royal heir as "Deceased (presumed). Remains: not on file." Agnes frowns at it: "Irregular." It means nothing to the player yet. In the ending, she stamps it "HEIR: ALIVE. FILE COMPLETE."

**Mortimer's fate.** Removed from office, he takes a number in the Waiting Room of Eternity, right behind Gerald.

**Freed state:** the queue machines stop, Gerald's number is finally called, and Agnes reorganizes the Department around one rule: help people. Prudence Pallor becomes her deputy.

## The Verdant Dump: nothing grows unless you let something go

**Core lesson:** death isn't the end, just a new beginning: a new adventure for those who have departed, and a new start for those yet to come. To grow, we have to be able to let things go.

**The situation.** Archdruid Compostella was corrupted through her own bond with nature, and the corruption spread through the ecosystem. The Verdant Dump isn't dying. It's unnaturally alive: plants that never stop growing, mutated fungi, roots splitting machinery. Nothing rots, so nothing feeds the soil, so everything chokes.

### Story beats

1. **Moss Mulligan (town gate).** A young faun druid with thorns in her arms, whispering for the "hungry green." Freed, she's shy, earnest, and loves raccoons.
2. **The zone.** Junkyard-farm comedy (smug raccoons, goats that eat the signs) gives way to overgrowth that's wrong in a way the druids can feel. Brother Bramble notices first, and is also furious about whoever keeps eating the compost.
3. **The Rotheart.** The deeper the player goes, the more warped the land. At the Heart Roots the player severs the tendrils feeding the corruption, which weakens the boss.
4. **Compostella, the Rotheart.** She speaks in the many voices of the hungry green, sorrowful and enraged.
5. **The cure.** To free her, the player must let the corrupted thing at the heart of the grove finally die. Compostella has to agree to it.

**Why her bond turned.** After a great loss (the ancient tree she grew up beneath), Primm brought Compostella a gift: a seed engineered by the Path-ologists, life that never dies. In her grief she planted it. It is the Corrupted Seed whose records sit in the Castle's Refusemancer wing. Her line at the end: "Everything I ever loved, I gave back to the soil. Except this one. I couldn't." Letting it rot is the first honest compost in ten years.

**Compostella to the Wanderer:** "You're carrying a loss you can't name yet. When you find its name, don't bury it in a jar. Death isn't the end of them. Let it be the start of something in you." It echoes Agnes, who stays to help before she finally moves on.

**Freed state:** the overgrowth recedes, the spores fade, the Dump smells like honest compost again, and the raccoons are finally put on trial.

## Primm's Perfection (the Capital) and Primm's Castle

**Core idea:** the kingdom's heart, where the cost of perfection is visible everywhere, and where the Wanderer's story and the kingdom's story become the same story.

### The Capital: Primm's Perfection

Primm renamed the Capital after himself: **Primm's Perfection**. It becomes **Pathordia** after his fall.

- **Getting in.** Reachable from the start. Beat Captain Spotless at the gate (absurd entry rules, a hard fight), or find the Old Service Tunnels through Kestrel's quest. The tunnels were built by all four Paths together: the old harmony, literally underneath Primm's city.
- **The Showcase Quarter.** The eerie facade district at the city's center (previously called "Primm's Perfection" in the build): identical houses, fixed smiles, loudspeakers, decree signs, citizens who whisper "help."
- **Beyond the facade.** Cracked, gray, coming apart, and torn by rifts. The rifts exist because Primm's forced connection to the Paths has been failing since the prince escaped.
- **The broken services.** Each unfreed Path leaves a debuff on the Capital; each freed zone lifts one and visibly restores something.
- **The resistance.** Wren leads it from the hideout, with Fig Sly's black market. Elder Maren is its contact in Crosspath, and she has been quietly turning Path-ologists inside the lab; the rescuer is one of them.
- **The four Path quests.** Widow Pell (Necrocrat), Rollo Spokes (Beefcake), Chef Marlo (Gourmand), and Old Fern (Refusemancer) each show what Primm's rule costs ordinary people, and each reveals one insight into Primm.
- **The hunt.** Enforcers search for an "escaped Royal Asset," with posters that read like lost-property notices ("Have you seen this Improvement Opportunity?"). Funny at first, chilling after the fourth memory.

### Primm's Castle

The existing layout stays (Portrait Gallery, Ministry of Correction, Hall of Mirrors, the four faction wings, Correction Ward, Archive of Good Intentions, the Model Room). Story beats:

- **Primm's Private Gallery:** the royal family portrait, the king and queen painted over in white. Only the boy's face is left, "kept for reference."
- **The Path-ologists' records:** notes on "the Subject" reveal what was done to the prince, and how the stolen power holds Primm on the throne. Some of the oldest notes are in Maren's handwriting.
- **The Archive of Good Intentions:** Primm's early journals, sincere and helpful, sliding entry by entry into control.

### The final fight

1. **Standardization:** everyone forced into the same shape.
2. **Reflection:** he copies the Wanderer's cards. "Everything you can do, I can do. I've had ten years of practice with it." His power is literally the prince's.
3. **Unraveling:** his rules break, the arena cracks, the selfishness under the sincerity shows.

Freed leaders lend boons for every completed zone: Grandmaster Flex, the Grand Chef, Agnes Overdue, and Archdruid Compostella.

**If the player hasn't freed all four zones,** the Wanderer doesn't know who he is yet, so Primm tells him: "You don't even know, do you? I kept your face." Rushing the castle becomes its own darker version of the reveal.

**His answer to Primm** draws on the zone lessons he has learned: strength isn't control, a name isn't a self, no order outranks treating people with respect, and nothing grows if you refuse to let anything go.

## Ending and postgame

### The ending

1. **The Pathwork Throne.** Primm falls. The prince takes his place on the throne, and it answers to the true bloodline. His memory and power return in full.
2. **The kingdom heals.** The facade crumbles, the rifts close, and the four Paths reconnect, not forced into one shape but carried together, the way the royal family always held them. Primm's Perfection is renamed Pathordia.
3. **Primm sees it.** As balance returns to the Paths, Primm finally sees the kingdom as it is rather than as his reflection, and understands how much damage he caused. He still has some valid points, but he cannot be trusted.
4. **The reunion.** The freed leaders gather. Agnes stamps the transfer file "HEIR: ALIVE. FILE COMPLETE." Maren finally calls him Tessar.
5. **The unlock.** He can now wield every Path at once, so the player can build 3+ Path decks. The deckbuilding unlock IS the story's resolution.
6. **Return to the world.** Pathordia is changed, citizens are free, and the postgame continues.

**Primm's fate.** The prince doesn't kill him; Primm made one choice for everyone, and the prince makes a different one. Primm agrees to serve the kingdom doing what he does best, infrastructure and technology improvements, under permanent arrest and kept away from all Path workings. Brilliant, sincere, and never again in charge.

### Postgame: the Path-ology Lab

- **Rip's tip.** After Primm's defeat, Rip Tearson tells the Wanderer he was exploring the forest where it all started and saw some interesting things. He's opened a portal station there, now on the portal network.
- **The hidden entrance.** Near where the Wanderer woke (inaccessible until now) is the entrance to the Path-ology Lab, where he was held for ten years.
- **The Path-ology Lab.** A postgame dungeon of extremely challenging encounters: failed experiments, Path-ologist guards, and the chamber where his power was drained.
- **His cell.** A story node with ten years of tally marks on the wall, and a child's drawing of four colors.
- **The Chief Path-ologist.** Dr. Ambrose Siphon, Maren's former protege, who took over her research when she fled. A named character and the lab's boss, still running experiments without Primm.
- **The rescuer.** The defected Path-ologist Maren turned. The rescuer was caught after the escape and is being held in the lab; freeing them is the dungeon's heart, and it's what Rip's "interesting things" were. Name undecided (use the placeholder "The Rescuer" in one data location).
