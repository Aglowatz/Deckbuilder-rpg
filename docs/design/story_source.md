## Gourmands — The Endless Buffet / The Test Kitchen
The Gourmands' leader has been secretly replaced by a doppelgänger working for the big bad. The impostor has maintained the appearance and authority of the original leader while gradually transforming the Endless Buffet into a massive R&D operation. The doppelgänger is using magically corrupted food to influence and corrupt the Gourmands, turning their natural love of food, invention, and feeding others against them. Beneath the surface, the Gourmands are being forced to develop enormous food-powered war machines and magical technology for the big bad. The heroes eventually discover that what looks like a thriving center of culinary innovation is actually a military-industrial complex.

Dungeon: The Test Kitchen. The Test Kitchen is the Gourmands' massive scientific and technological R&D facility. It combines the aesthetic of an experimental kitchen, food laboratory, and ridiculous weapons-development center. The heroes fight through increasingly dangerous prototypes, corrupted ingredients, experimental food constructs, and enormous unfinished war machines before confronting the doppelgänger leader.

## Beefcakes — The Gainlands / The House of Gains
The original Beefcake leader was violently overthrown by the big bad's forces and imprisoned beneath the House of Gains. His captors deliberately deprived him of everything they believed made a Beefcake strong: weights, protein, gym equipment, and conventional training resources. They assumed that without these things he would eventually become weak and broken. Instead, he turned to calisthenics, yoga, stretching, meditation, and mental discipline, becoming stronger despite his imprisonment. The leader initially appears to the heroes as weak, emaciated, and decrepit, but after being rescued he joins them in confronting the bad guy. At the confrontation, he throws off his outer clothing to reveal that he is still incredibly muscular. He explains that true strength isn't simply the ability to lift heavy things—it comes from the heart and the mind. The villain took away everything he thought created strength, but inadvertently left the leader with the opportunity to discover a deeper kind of strength.

Dungeon: The House of Gains. The House of Gains is the primary Beefcake dungeon and the stronghold of the regime that has taken control of the zone. Somewhere within it lies a major section called the Iron-less Prison, where the original leader has been held. The heroes must descend into the prison, rescue the leader, and then continue through the House of Gains to confront the current ruler/bad guy.

## Necrocrats — The Department of Necrotic Affairs / The Hall of Final Approvals
The Necrocrats aren't inherently good or evil—they simply believe that death should be properly administered according to policy. The big bad exploited that philosophy rather than trying to corrupt them. Through a completely legitimate bureaucratic process, the necessary paperwork was submitted, reviewed, approved, notarized, and filed, transferring authority over the Necrocrat government to the big bad. As far as the Necrocrats are concerned, the paperwork is valid, so they are now legally obligated to follow the new leadership. Even when the heroes point out that the big bad is clearly evil and is causing immense suffering, the Necrocrats' response is essentially, "Yes, but the authorization is valid." The resulting oppression is therefore absurdly bureaucratic: citizens are trapped beneath endless rules, permits, approvals, and procedures, while the Necrocrats dutifully enforce them because that's what the policy says.

Dungeon: The Hall of Final Approvals. The Hall of Final Approvals is the ultimate bureaucratic nightmare—a massive government complex where every aspect of life, death, resurrection, and the afterlife requires authorization. The heroes must navigate increasingly ridiculous departments, paperwork, waiting rooms, regulations, and undead bureaucrats while trying to reach the authority that now controls the Necrocrats.

## Refusemancers — The Verdant Dump / The Rotheart
The Refusemancers possess an extraordinarily powerful magical connection to nature and the living world. Their entire civilization is built around cycles of decay, regeneration, recycling, agriculture, and transformation. The big bad discovered that this connection could be turned against them. Somehow, the Refusemancer leader became corrupted through their connection to nature itself, and the corruption began spreading through the magical ecosystem. The natural world around the Verdant Dump isn't simply dying—it is becoming unnaturally alive and twisted. Plants grow uncontrollably, fungi mutate, roots invade structures, and the normal cycles of life, death, and regeneration become distorted. As the corruption spreads, the residents suffer because the very ecosystem they depend upon has become hostile and diseased.

Dungeon: The Rotheart. The Rotheart lies deep within the corrupted ecosystem, at the source of the magical infection. The deeper the heroes travel, the more they encounter a warped version of the Verdant Dump's normally vibrant natural environment. Eventually they reach the Rotheart, the corrupted core connecting the leader to the larger ecosystem, where they must confront the Refusemancer leader and sever the big bad's influence over the natural world.

## Alchemist and crafting cards
The alchemist will be our crafting vendor that allows us to craft multicolor spells. The alchemist gets unlocked after 2 zones are completed.

Whenever a player obtains over 4 copies of a card, that player obtains a little bit of "essence" for the card's respective path. The higher rarity, the more "essence" they will obtain. Once a player has enough "essence" for two Paths, they can go to the alchemist and trade in all of their "essence" for those two Paths, plus some gold, and they will get a random multipath card from those two Paths.

At first only dual path cards can be created, but once you beat the main boss of the story, you can create tri path cards (maybe 4 path cards). For now, just create 4 multicolor spells for each dual path. Do not worry about 3 or 4 path cards.
