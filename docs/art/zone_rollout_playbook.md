# Zone rollout playbook

The exact process used to dress the Gainlands, the D.N.A., the Endless Buffet and the Verdant Dump with their approved zone kits (`docs/art/gainlands_slice.md`, `docs/art/dna_slice.md`), written down so the next zones (Endless Buffet, Verdant Dump, the Capital outside and inside) follow the same recipe. Each step names the files, the commands and the check that closes it. One Godot process at a time; GUT after every part; e2e only for the area changed, about 15 minutes a part.

**There are no sub-areas** (Art Bible G-07, `docs/design/subarea_removal.md`): the only walkable areas are the forest, the town, the four Path zones and the Capital, each one scene; variety comes from regions within that scene, and every dungeon (main, mini, side) is node-based from an entrance in the zone. Primm's Castle and the Path-ology Lab have no kit of their own (node maps, battleboards and backdrops only). A zone pass dresses the one main scene: its regions, its region landmarks and its distant views.

The rules that never change: gameplay, quests, NPC logic, dungeon entrances and the camera system are not touched by an art pass; the Drive is read-only (every importer copies); nothing in the world carries readable text (Art Bible G-03); characters stay the clearest thing on screen; Medium holds 60 fps on the dev iGPU.

## 0. Before the kit: paint-overs and the kit list

1. **Paint-over inputs.** `Godot --path . --resolution 1600x900 -s res://tools/zone_shots.gd -- --area=<area> --mode=both` writes `docs/art/paintover_inputs/<area>/PO-<AREA>-HUB.png` (the area from its arrival camera, Cinematic / Normal, the hero visible, no UI) and the 9-combination contact sheet `docs/art/zone_plans_shots/<area>.png`. A region of the zone that deserves its own paint-over is shot with `bash tools/shot.sh res://scenes/<scene>.tscn PO-<AREA>-<REGION> --at=<anchor> --nohud=true` from a named anchor, so the finished paint-over and the game shot line up.
2. **The kit list.** `docs/art/zone_kits/<area>.md` is the sheet-ready table (ID, Asset, Category, Lock, Image Description, Size / Format, In-Game Use, Priority): the Art Bible budget per zone is about 40 images in six locks (SURFACE, CUTOUT, TREE, SHEET, CONCEPT, BACKDROP). It is pasted into the designer's sheet; the export comes back as `data/source/<area>_kit.csv` (rows 1-7 the style target and locks, row 9 the header, assets from row 10, like `data/source/env_kit.csv`).
3. **The art** lands in `G:\My Drive\Card Game Art\Approved_ZoneKits\<Zone>` named by ID (`GAIN-WALL-BRICK.png`). Paint-overs go to `Paintovers/PAINTOVER-<AREA>-<REGION>.png` (all of them show regions of the one main zone) and are copied into `docs/art/paintovers/`.

## 1. Import (Part A)

4. `bash tools/import_zone_kit.sh <area>` ("import zone kit <area>"): copies and prepares every id of the sheet by lock (surfaces: seam check and repair, normal and roughness maps, 400 px/m like the town kit; cut-outs and trees: alpha cleanup, trim, pivot; sheets: sliced into `<ID>-01..06`; concepts: small references under `docs/art/zone_kits/<area>/concepts/`; backdrops: full width), writes the registry `data/art/zone_kits/<area>.json` and the report `docs/art/zone_kits/<area>_import_report.md`. **Read the report first**: missing ids, files that match no id, and notes (an opaque "sky", an empty sheet cell) are the first thing to tell the user.
5. **Check the slices and pivots** by eye: a strip of each sheet's six parts (`_screenshots/zonekit_<SHEET>.png` in the Gainlands run) and the kit bench with the zone's swaps: `bash tools/shot.sh res://tools/kit_bench.tscn <name> --presets=cottage,inn --trees=oak,pine --zone=<area> --nohud=true`.
6. **Hand numbers in the registry** (they survive a re-import): `height` for prop and plant cards (metres), `width` for sheet windows, aliases for painted textures the zone reuses (`"placeholder": "tex:<name>"`, `"dec:<name>"`).
7. **Hero landmarks.** For each CONCEPT id write a static builder in `world/<area>/<area>_landmarks.gd` (`has_model`, `build(spec)`), register the prefix in `HeroLandmarks.BUILDERS`, model it to the `size` the zone builder asks for with `KitMesh` primitives textured from the kit (`EnvKit.material(id)`), painted cards for banners, signs, doors, windows and plants (`_card`, `_tree_card`), and set meta `circles` (blockers), `spinner` (a part that turns), `lights`, `no_wind`. Check each one alone: `bash tools/shot.sh res://tools/kit_bench.tscn lm --landmarks=<ID>:<w>x<h>x<d> --zone=<area> --dist=16 --zoom=2.6 --lyaw=25`. The size comes from where it stands in the main zone (a region landmark must keep the declutter clearances of section 5; a distant view may be as big as the concept); the silhouette from the concept. Place it from the zone builder (`_place_landmark` / `_far_landmark` in `GainlandsBuilder` and `DnaBuilder` are the patterns: on the root, `no_range_cull`, its circles solid, or a view off the floor with `no_occlusion_fade` too).
8. **Backdrops and sky.** Backdrop strips become `kit_cards` in the zone's mood (`data/art/zone_looks.json`, scenery block: id, compass angle, angular width, ring, lift). A real sky panorama would replace the painted gradient; an opaque picture stays a reference (GK-2).
9. **Tests, commit, push.** `bash tools/run_tests.sh`; `tests/world/test_zone_kit.gd` covers the slicer, pivots, families, the registry and the swaps; add the zone's ids to it.

## 2. Dress (Part B)

10. **Material swaps** in `data/zone_styles/<area>.json`: every shared kit id the zone's buildings, paths and trees use maps to a zone id (walls, roofs, eave trim, doors, windows, signs, ground, trees). Zone art is untinted (`tinted: false`); shared surfaces the zone keeps (cobble, foundation, fences) still take the palette tints.
11. **Building presets** in `data/art/building_presets.json`: a few `<area>_*` presets with the zone walls, roof, trim and sliced doors / windows / signs (storeys 1.2-1.9 heroes, `timber: none` when the wall paints its own posts).
12. **Prop clusters** in `data/art/zone_kits/<area>_clusters.json` (`ZoneClusters`): vignettes of the kit's card props and plants with shared kit props, named like `PropClusters` names and placed by the zone's dressing. Card props anywhere: `PropKit.make("card:<ID>")`. Plant kinds for scatter: `TreeCards.KINDS` (`plant: true`).
13. **The regions**: each region of the one scene gets its own ground, clusters at doorways / walls / junctions, flower clumps along its paths, kit decals (flat quads: chalk, plates, ruts) and its region landmark or distant view, all from the zone's layout, builder and dressing (`<Zone>Layout`, `<Zone>Builder`, `<Zone>Dressing`); the generic `ZoneDressing` scatter is off for a zone that dresses itself. Keep every anchor, chest, enemy home, puzzle and entrance position exactly as it was.
14. **The hub**: the zone's own builder swaps its procedural materials to the kit (`<Area>Materials.kit(...)`), its layout trees to painted cards (`KitFoliage.tree_field`), its stand-in building to the hero model (`LandmarkKit.build({"id": ...})`), and its dressing places kit clusters at the anchors (`KIT_CLUSTERS`). Layout positions and obstacles stay.
15. **Check**: the area at the paint-over camera (`zone_shots.gd --mode=po --out=res://_screenshots/<area>_slice`) and each region from its anchor (`tools/shot.sh --at=<anchor>`), `bash tools/fps.sh <scene> 1` (Medium, 60), the zone's walk smoke (`tools/run_<area>_walk_smoke.sh`) and the walkability probe, GUT, commit, push.

## 3. Match the paint-overs (Part C)

16. **Numbers first**: `Godot --headless -s res://tools/image_stats.gd -- <paint-over> <shot> ...` gives mean RGB, luma p5 / p50 / p95, saturation and the sky / mid / ground thirds of both; grade is data (`data/zone_styles/<area>.json`: key, ambient, contrast, saturation, post saturation, vignette, haze). Three passes at least: (1) composition and landmark size, (2) building shapes, prop density and palette, (3) light, shadows and haze. Re-shoot after each pass; keep the hero the most saturated thing on screen.
17. **Side by side**: `tools/side_by_side.gd --left=<paint-over> --right=<shot> --out=docs/art/<area>_slice/compare_<sub>.jpg --height=450`; the hub's 9 combinations: `zone_shots.gd --mode=grid --out=...` (every combination must look finished, G-02).
18. **fps** after every pass; batch anything repeated (cards in MultiMeshes, `KitBatch`, `KitPools`), keep real lights to the lantern budget.

## 4. Report (Part D)

19. `docs/art/<area>_slice.md`: paint-over beside the result for every scene, the hub contact sheet, fps per scene, the grade numbers, the honest list of what still does not match, a sheet-ready table of extra assets that would close the gap, and the kit status table (`data/art/zone_kits/<area>.json`). Update `docs/art/art_status.md` (the kit ids now in the game), `docs/design/open_questions.md` (judgment calls), `docs/progress.md`.

## 5. The declutter rules (apply from the start; learned in the Gainlands corrections and the D.N.A. slice)

Every zone follows these before it is dressed (GC-1 .. GC-10, DN-5 .. DN-8); the tests of `tests/world/test_gainlands_corrections.gd` and `tests/world/test_dna_corrections.gd` are the pattern to copy.

20. **Routes**: main routes at least four hero widths wide (4.2 m roads outdoors, `LINK_WIDTH` 4 cells in a grid plan) with nothing solid inside 2.4 m of an edge; spurs 3.4-3.6 m.
21. **Breathing room**: 5.5 m of open ground round every NPC, vendor, gate, chest, puzzle, travel point and interactable; interactables at least 2.5 m from each other so their prompts never fight (the barista and the coffee machine, DN-13).
22. **Clusters, never scatter**: props in a few tidy clusters at walls, doorways and junctions (`<zone>_clusters.json`, placed by a `<Zone>Dressing` that refuses the plaza middle, the routes, the entrance and every anchor's reach); the generic `ZoneDressing` furniture scatter is off for a zone that has its own; flowers only in clumps along path edges; boards only where they label something (no posters, no memos).
23. **The hub**: an empty central plaza (`PLAZA_RADIUS` outdoors, the lobby middle in a grid plan) with the hub's things spread round its rim, 4 m or more apart, each facing the plaza.
24. **Camera occlusion**: smooth transparency, never a dither. Terrain, floors and knee-high walls carry `no_occlusion_fade`; painted cards fade themselves per card (`kit_card.gdshader`); hundreds of small solid pieces in one chunk mesh fade per piece in their own shader (`world/dna/dna_fade.gdshader`, `DnaFadeMesh`: the piece's centre, half width and top in CUSTOM0, the hero position set each frame); buildings fade as a group through `OcclusionFader`. An indoor-feeling area never hides the hero: either knee-high (0.5 m) balustrades, or (the D.N.A. since Oct 19, `DnaInterior`) full-height walls built as per-piece fading pieces whose span is tested against the sight line and which cut away wide near the lens (`near_reach`).
25. **The entrance is walked**: a bridge or an approach causeway with a gate, arrival on it facing the zone, the exit spot at its far end and walking off the end leaves for town (armed only after the hero has been inside); the town passage has no far glow for that zone; no spinning disc anywhere.
26. **The goal you can see**: the main dungeon is a huge hero model (the House of Gains 48 x 34 x 30 m, the Hall of Final Approvals 64 x 40 x 24 m) beyond the map's edge, hanging off the root (never chunk-culled) with every piece `no_range_cull` and `no_occlusion_fade`; a proper gate or stair at the spot, an icon board beside it, `no_plate` on the spot (the HUD prompt names it), no placeholder blocks, cones, boulders or lettering; it stands as a far landmark that the regions facing it see across the map.
27. **Every route walked**: the walkability probe (`tests/world/test_walkability_zones.gd`) and a windowed walk smoke with real input (`tools/run_gainlands_walk_smoke.sh`, `tools/run_dna_walk_smoke.sh`: arrival, hub, every NPC and interactable, the dungeon entrances, the locked landmarks and the way out), which reads any dialogue after a teleport and sidesteps when a straight walk is blocked.
28. **Region landmarks and views** (SR-8): a landmark kept inside the main scene is either a region landmark on walkable ground (its blocking circles 2.4 m off every route edge, out of the plaza, 5.5 m off every anchor; a flat spot and a keep-clear circle in the layout so no scattered tree or rock stands under it; shrink the model before crowding anything) or a distant view beyond the walkable floor (off the land, past the balustrade, on a floating islet; `no_range_cull`, `no_occlusion_fade`, nothing solid). A test per zone checks it (`test_the_region_landmarks_stand_clear...`, `test_the_far_landmarks_are_views...`).
29. **Rewards inside the zone** (SR-7): a zone may hold a shrine (spot kind `shrine`, `ZoneShrine`: one offering per campaign from the zone's own chest table) and lore boards (pictogram signs whose words live in the story file); never a new economy, never at the cost of clutter.

## 7. Lessons of the Endless Buffet slice (BF-1 ... in `docs/design/open_questions.md`)

30. **Count the dungeons before placing the entrance landmarks.** A zone has a main dungeon and a side dungeon; the "mini dungeon" spot of a zone IS the side dungeon's door (`ZoneScene._ask_mini_dungeon` through `DungeonCatalog.side_for_zone`). The Buffet's Walk-In Freezer door opens the Omakase, so the Omakase Pavilion stands over the freezer door; a brief that names three entrances means two (BF-2).
31. **The paint-overs may be in the kit folder.** Look in `Approved_ZoneKits/<Zone>` as well as `Paintovers/`; the importer lists them as unmatched files, which is right (BF-1).
32. **Check the world look's sea plane against the ground heights** (`WorldScenery.WATER_Y` -0.36): a table whose mash dips below it is flooded; turn `scenery.water` off for that mood and put the water where the paint-over wants it (the Buffet's moat under the bridge) (BF-3).
33. **A region landmark that cannot stand inside the table becomes a view where its story lands**: Potato Peak beyond the rim where the river leaves the table, so its streams feed the river (BF-5). Give every region landmark a flat kept-clear spot AND write its blocking circle into the layout (`LANDMARK_BLOCKERS`) so the layout-only readers (the walkability probe, the chest scan, the district gate tests) see it; a plinth that takes over a wall line keeps the posts just outside it (BF-6).
34. **Gameplay walls can lose their poles**: a picket line of props stays as layout data (same blockers, same gaps) and is drawn as one knee-high wall mesh built from the post positions (`BuffetLandmarks.district_walls`) (BF-11). Crossings that are mechanics (rafts, a turntable) keep their mechanics and get landing stages on both banks instead of a bridge (BF-10).
35. **A backdrop card on the near ring at 96 degrees swallows the sky**: a tiling skyline goes on the far ring at about 60 degrees per card (BF-7).
36. **Moving the spawn onto a bridge changes what the smokes assume**: the core zone test's flood grid must reach the bridge, the "reachable" helper must accept the bridge surface, the minimap check at arrival sees the way in rather than the hub, and the paint-over camera becomes a named plaza anchor (`tools/zone_shots.gd`) (BF-16). A dressing piece that stands on a jelly pad (or any pad, chest, pickup) shows up only in the e2e smoke: keep the pads in the keep-clear list of every dressing table.
37. **Side dungeons are sealed until their quest**: an e2e step that clicks the old "Open the door" button fails on the sealed toast; set the unlock flag like the D.N.A. smoke and click "Enter" (BF-13).
38. **Paint-over cameras as layout data, not anchors** (`BuffetLayout.PO_CAMERAS`, `--at=po_<region>`): anchors are walkability destinations and keep-clear spots; a camera spot is neither.

## 8. Lessons of the Verdant Dump layout pass (VD-1 ... in `docs/design/open_questions.md`)

39. **Fix the layout before the kit exists.** A zone with no art yet still gets the whole of section 5 first: regions as layout data (`REGION_IDS`, several circles per region), the bridge, the plaza, the routes, the clusters, the boards, stand-ins of the planned sizes with their circles in `LANDMARK_BLOCKERS`, and a slice test that enforces every rule (`tests/world/test_heap_slice.gd` is the pattern); the paint-over inputs are then shot from `PO_CAMERAS` that the art pass reuses.
40. **Count the dungeons again** (VD-1, as BF-2): the "mini dungeon" spot opens the side dungeon; the stand-in of the side dungeon's landmark stands over that spot.
41. **A door may move to give a region its own ground** when the thing that really gates it is a quest flag, not the layout (VD-2): say so in the reachability tests.
42. **The sea plane floods any zone whose land rolls below -0.36** (VD-4, BF-3): check `scenery.water` for every zone before shooting.
43. **Camera spots are not interact spots** (VD-10): a paint-over camera stands a spot radius off every spot (the near-spot plate shows otherwise), out of the fade reach of the tall piece behind it, and off every enemy home; `tools/screenshot.gd --clean=true` hides the plates and markers.
44. **The corruption gradient before the art is data** (VD-8): `corruption` in the zone style read by the layout (ground tint and darkening by position, flower density), not a second mood.

## 9. Lessons of the Verdant Dump art pass (VD-11 ... in `docs/design/open_questions.md`)

45. **The sheet and the folder may not exist as named.** No `data/source/<zone>_kit.csv`: rebuild it from the kit plan and the delivered files (ids as delivered, descriptions from the pictures); a Drive folder not named after the area id is mapped in `art_config.cfg` (`zonekit_folders`). Read the delivered list against the plan: ids may be missing or new (VD-11).
46. **Check the measured pivots of trees**: a stray leaf at a card's base pulls the trunk off; a `pivot_hand` in the registry survives a re-import (VD-13). `KitMesh.blob` is circular in plan (it ignores radii.z); a long mound needs a real ellipsoid or a cone (VD-12).
47. **The goal's crown and the camera's sky**: the Cinematic camera shows about 11 degrees above the horizon, so a tall landmark near the zone shows only its trunk. Make a big tree low and wide (a weeping crown) so its underside frames the top of every view, or set it far; keep its door at its base (VD-14).
48. **A glowing hollow is a gradient, not an icon**: one fan of the shape with bright-core-to-dark-rim vertex colours on an unshaded material whose colour is the hue (pulse the colour, swap it for the freed state); soft lobes read as faces (VD-15).
49. **The corruption gradient technique** (the Dump's): one number per point, `corruption_at(x, z)` from data (`start_z`, `end_z`, `centre_x`, `spread`: a smoothstepped band strongest on the centre line), scaled by the freed state (`twist_at`). Every layer reads it with its own threshold, so the turn is gradual and the healthy-to-sick change happens where the story wants it: the vertex tint (light), the corrupted soil as a painted ground layer (`smoothstep(0.35, 0.95)`), decals by mask (veins where sick, petals where healthy), tree and plant kinds by threshold with a dithered band (`trees` 0.62, `trees_band`, `fungus` 0.6), flower density (layout), and the grade by where the hero stands (the look rig's `values` targets in 5 % steps with a `grade_curve`, the sun and ambient eased). Test it: no step above about 0.08 per metre, healthy hub and farm, deepest at the goal, and the freed build has none of it. Violet stays an accent (VD-17, VD-23).
50. **Build the freed state when the game has it** (`Session.is_zone_completed`): scale the gradient to nothing at build, give the ground preset a `freed` variant (a spare texture finds its use there), turn the goal's glow warm (VD-18). A zone has five painted ground layers and three prop special slots (shared with `atlas_override`); a fourth special silently takes the atlas override's slot (VD-19).
51. **Re-make old pieces in place** (VD-20): a zone without BuildingKit buildings gets static kit builders for its layout features (shacks, stalls, fences, bridges keeping their growth segments) at the same positions with the same blockers; ninety tyre stacks drawn as one fence mesh dropped 300 draw calls.
52. **The land must go on** (VD-21): a far ground 26 m down reads as a sea; put it just under the rim and grow woods beyond the sides, never on the south rim where the cameras stand.
53. **Measure fps by hiding, not guessing** (`HEAP_HIDE=<name,...>`, `OCCLUSION_OFF`, the probe's GPU ms): readings near 16.7 ms are bimodal, so a single A/B can mislead; the Dump's 3.5 ms was a faded ghost of the greenhouse between the trailing camera and the hero, fixed by hiding it while it blocks (VD-22). Kit ground surfaces need mipmaps (an import rule).
54. **Smokes go stale with the layout**: re-run the old zone smoke after a layout pass and either update it or log what is left (the eighth-brief Dump smoke, `docs/backlog.md`).

## 10. Lessons of the Capital layout pass (CAP-1 ... in `docs/design/open_questions.md`)

55. **Count what the camera can see above the horizon before sizing walls and goals.** The Cinematic camera (pitch 20, FOV 44) sees only about 6 degrees above the horizon: a 12 m wall 45 m away hides everything behind it. Work out the visible band at the goal's distance (camera height about 7.5 m plus distance x tan of the frame top) and put the goal's silhouette there; keep a near wall under it (the Capital: a 9 m wall, the castle's crenellations and cone roofs at 10-25 m) (CAP-4).
56. **A gameplay hub is not a region.** A hall reached by teleports (the Crease) keeps its gating (when it is found, where you wake); a brief's "camp" region is built as the walkable refuge it describes, and folding the two together is a gameplay brief (CAP-1).
57. **Routes as segments with widths** (`CapitalLayout.routes`, `route_edge_distance`, `rect_route_distance`) let a cell-grid zone be tested like the Dump: nothing solid on a route, buildings 2.4 m off, rifts and hazards 0.5 m off.
58. **A top-down plan is the fastest layout check** (`tools/capital_plan.gd`): cells, routes, circles, anchors, chests, cameras in one picture before any 3D is built. Load the layout with `load()` inside `_initialize`, not as a typed class: a `-s` script compiles before the autoloads exist.
59. **A long wall the camera trails behind**: fade it, then hide it once faded while it blocks the lens (`CapitalScene._hide_wall_round_the_lens`); the ghost cost 3.5 ms at the Transit Yards (VD-22 again).
60. **Strollers and prompts**: a wandering NPC's spot follows it, so keep each wanderer's home 5 m or more from other interact spots (the alley citizens and the perfect doors), and have smokes follow the spot, not the anchor.
61. **Walk smokes hold the roaming enemies still** when they bump the hero off scripted lines; the battles have their own smokes.

## 11. Lessons of the Capital art pass (CAP-12 ... in `docs/design/open_questions.md`)

62. **A zone whose id is not its name** (the Capital is zone `final`): map both the Drive folder (`zonekit_folders`) and the sheet (`zonekit_sheets`) in `art_config.cfg`; the registry, the art folder and the concepts then live under the zone id. **Check every file arrived whole**: a PNG cut short on the Drive (no IEND) loads as an error, and the rest of the run still imports; rebuild what is readable in the scratchpad, import it with `--only`, and ask for the re-upload (CAP-13).
63. **Sheets that say "crop me"**: a texture painted as bands for cropping becomes one tile per band, and a missing colour of a tile set can be derived (a hue turn), all in a tool that writes registry entries with `derived_from` (`tools/capital_kit_derive.gd`) so a re-import can redo them (CAP-14).
64. **The two-halves technique** (one scene, two moods): every look value exists twice, per half, as data (the zone style files `capital_outside` / `capital_inside`, the moods `capital_outskirts` / `capital_inside` and their blackout and freed variants), the style rig and the look rig switch at the wall (`CapitalScene._cross_gate`). Check the variants: the paint-overs may be shot in a variant (all four services broken = the blackout), and a variant preset that sets no exposure falls back to the default (the freed city blew out to glare, CAP-24). Keep the "bleak outside, perfect inside" rules as tests (`test_capital_mood`, `test_world_look_rig`: the outside's preset saturation under 0.8, its post saturation under the inside's); carry the paint-overs' colour in the post grade when a preset must stay "sickly".
65. **One landmark seen from both halves**: a far landmark that must read through one half's thick fog takes its own haze (`StyleToon.META_FAR_HAZE`: the toon shader rebuilt once from its own source with the fragment writing FOG from an instance uniform; the colour and density per half as data, `far_haze`, set at each crossing). Work out the visible band from each half's cameras (section 10) and put the landmark's readable parts there: its doors and emblem in the near band, its cones and spires in the band over the wall; lower anything of the wall that hides it (the gate's crown) (CAP-22, CAP-23).
66. **The district-state technique**: a district's broken / restored state is the Path zone's flag, read once at build (`_district_free`); each district model takes `state` and builds the matching half of its two-state concept on the layout building's footprint, and each district prop has a kit look per state (CAP-16, CAP-35). Test both states inside the footprint and different. Keep the quest's own states (`Off` / `On` groups) separate from the district's. The change is earned by a moment on the first return (CAP-34): hang it on the area-music change (every way in changes it: walking, the gate crossing, a passage straight into the district, a teleport), not on a timer, and mark it seen with a cosmetic flag nothing else reads; a smoke that frees the Paths must set the flags, not call `Session.complete_zone` from town (it plays the town's events), and must click "Continue" on a level-up box before waiting on timers (the box pauses the game).
67. **Dressing by sameness**: a "perfect" district is exact rows from the routes' data (one spacing, one offset, the same pieces), solid pieces 5.5 m off every spot and the route clearance off every edge, low non-solid pieces closer; record why each slot was refused (`CapitalDressing.refused`) so the density can be tuned from the numbers (CAP-30, CAP-38).
68. **Paint-overs that reframe**: when the painter moved the view, keep the cameras and match palette, light, haze, the landmarks' shapes and the dressing's density; say so in the report (CAP-36). Paint the ground per district from the paint-overs with the painted masks (lawn vs paving), not by changing the layout's floor kinds (CAP-37).
69. **Draw calls**: one `KitMesh` per model, shared materials cached (gold, mirror, water), repeated models flagged `kit_static` for `KitBatch`; a rift's real light with a 4-6 m reach, not 12 m (where several rifts share a view the wide reach cost 2-3 ms on Medium); a full-screen-texture lens per hazard is not worth its ghost (CAP-20, CAP-33).

## 12. Lessons of the Verdant Dump v2 rebuild (VD-01 ... in `docs/art/verdant_dump_v2.md`)

70. **Verticality on a one-height floor**: terraces as flat tiers with wavy edges and a 1.6 m cliff band below each (`Surface.CLIFF`, the plain height lerping across it so the mesh shows a face); the ways between them as rectangles with a height from one end to the other (`HeapLayout.Ramp`: stairs stepped in `height_at` so the stone treads match the walk, ramps, short steep vine ladders, a mount-only scree slope); bridges as a-to-b strips whose deck arches (`HeapLayout.Bridge.deck_height`), hazard underneath, abutments flat; nothing walkable under a bridge or a ledge. Flood-fill the gating after every change: the first fill walked round a barricade on level ground beside a stair's foot (make stair and ramp sides cliffs all along, VD-08) and found a ramp landing in a pocket a bean bridge was meant to gate (VD-09).
71. **Narrow and deep, not a table**: a zone that must not echo another drops the shared skeleton (the same rounded table, the river at the same z with crossings at the same x, the fence with gaps at the same x, districts side by side) before anything else; keep the goal's coordinates and grow the plate the other way (VD-02).
72. **The low camera and a ravine**: a cut narrower than about 8 m and deeper than 2.5 m hides its water behind the near lip from the Cinematic camera (VD-10); check the sightline from the lip before deciding the depth.
73. **Water at several levels** needs a far ground below the lowest water, and a south rim that steps gently down to the meadow instead of a drop, or the plane hides the river and the pool floats in a crater.
74. **No new art, still a new palette**: tint the mood (fog, sky gradient under the panorama, ambient) rather than textures; build what the kit lacks from `KitMesh` in flat palette colours (purple trees as shaded lobes, blossom beds, vines, wheels, stairs); glow slots blow out to white on big blobs; multiplying a green card by violet gives grey (VD-12).
75. **Density costs fill**: every added mesh family goes through `HEAP_HIDE` A/B at the busiest camera; small decoration casts no shadow and may range-cull (keep `no_occlusion_fade`), the terrain beyond the walls samples coarser on a grid aligned to the fine one, and the reading round 16.7 ms is bimodal (probe twice).
76. **Ambient life without new NPC rows**: critters on two-point runs, particle bees and petals, birds over the water, and workers as existing models whose lines live in the zone story file (`NpcBarks.register_lines`), never new entries in the imported NPC list (VD-14).
